iWhat it reads

Each kind of document, for what it knows.

Long sections — chainages, ground and invert levels, pipe sizes and materials, air valves and washouts
Pump data sheets and curves — duty, shut-off and run-out points, speed, inertia; curves fitted, or digitised by hand from any graph
Plan drawings (DXF) — whole networks: pipes with their bends, levels and sizes from the texts, devices from the symbols, metres or feet
Schedules in Excel or CSV — node and pipe tables, read by header rules without AI at all
Scanned plans — items read by the AI, scaled from the lengths written on the page
iiProtection, designed and tested

An air valve plan that has been through the surge.

Air valves at high points, grade changes, long runs and pump discharges, by AWWA M51 practice — sized for draining and filling, applied by splitting the pipes, then run in the surge. Where a valve slams, it becomes an anti-slam valve. The air vessel is then sized with the plan in place.

iiiThe copilot

It works the model the way you do.

The copilot uses the same commands as the interface: it builds and edits the model, runs the engines and explains what they found. Every change it makes is an undo step, and every number it quotes comes from the engines.
